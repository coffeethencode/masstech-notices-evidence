## The problem

There is **no § 1983 case against MassTech**. Everything below is a proxy from
other Massachusetts quasi-public bodies. MassTech is a body politic under
G.L. c. 40J, so before any right-to-record or Open Meeting Law claim reaches
the merits, three threshold questions have to be answered:

1. Is MassTech an **arm of the state** (Eleventh Amendment immunity)?
2. Is it a **"person"** amenable to suit under § 1983 (*Will v. Mich. Dep't of
   State Police*, 491 U.S. 58 (1989))?
3. If it is an arm, what **remedy** survives?

Arm-of-state status is what decides entity damages, *Monell* liability, and
whether a state-law claim such as G.L. c. 30A § 20(f) can ride along in
federal court at all (*Pennhurst*).

## Favorable authority

### *Doyle v. Dukakis*, 687 F. Supp. 18 (D. Mass. 1988) — **the closest fit**

**[CITE-OK]** — D. Mass., No. 85-0100-Y, Young, J., June 2, 1988. This
session confirmed the opinion frames its issue as whether the Massachusetts
Housing Finance Agency is "an arm [or alter ego] of the State partaking of the
State's Eleventh Amendment immunity, or is instead to be treated as a
municipal entity." **The result was not confirmed here.**

Per peer: **MHFA is NOT an arm of the state**; First Amendment retaliation
claim under § 1983. The peer's key point is that **MHFA's enabling statute is
near-verbatim c. 40J § 3** — which is what makes this the strongest available
analogue for MassTech rather than a loose comparison.

Related opinions in the same case, worth pulling together:
*Doyle v. Dukakis*, 634 F. Supp. 1441 (D. Mass. 1986) and 699 F. Supp. 357
(D. Mass. 1988).

### *Brox v. Woods Hole, Martha's Vineyard & Nantucket S.S. Auth.*

**[CITE-OK] — and corrected:** the peer gave only the docket. The reporter
cite is **83 F.4th 87 (1st Cir. 2023)**, No. 22-1267, decided Oct. 6, 2023.
Underlying dispute: the Authority's COVID-19 employee vaccination policy,
modeled on a Massachusetts executive order.

Peer reports (verified by peer from the PDF, **not here**): below, the
Authority argued it was "an agency of State government" and therefore not a
§ 1983 "person" under *Will*. On appeal it **conceded** — "on all the present
facts and circumstances, the [Authority] was not acting as an arm-of-the-state
relative to this matter" — and the court held: "Thus, *Will* poses no bar to
the appellants' § 1983 claims ... and the Eleventh Amendment poses no bar to
their state-law claims."

**Read this narrowly.** It is a **concession, not a holding**, with no
analysis. Its value is twofold: (a) the context-specific framing — arm-of-state
status assessed *relative to the matter*, not as a fixed attribute of the
entity; and (b) it cleared *Will* **and** *Pennhurst* in one move, which is the
precise mechanism by which a § 20(f) state-law claim stays in federal court.

### *Morrison-Knudsen Co. v. MBTA*, 573 F. Supp. 698 (D. Idaho 1983)

**[CITE-OK]** — D. Idaho, No. 83-1124, Ryan, J., Nov. 7, 1983. Peer verified
from the CourtListener text (**not here**): "The Authority is not a dependent
arm of the Commonwealth ... does not partake in the protection afforded the
Commonwealth under the eleventh amendment."

Weak authority — a **diversity contract case**, out-of-district, 1983. Useful
for the proposition, not for persuasion.

## Adverse authority — and how to distinguish it

Peer's framing: these are **treasury-funded bodies or line agencies**, which
is the distinction to press.

- *Surprenant* (MassDOT)
- *Wojcik v. Mass. State Lottery Comm'n*, 300 F.3d 92 (1st Cir. 2002)
- *Willette* (UMass), 812 F.3d 35 (1st Cir. 2016)
- *Wampanoag Tribe v. MCAD*, 63 F. Supp. 2d 119 (D. Mass. 1999)

**Not** Eleventh Amendment holdings, despite the language: Massport "arm of the
state" talk in *Interface Group* (a *Parker* antitrust case) and in
*Jalbert Leasing*, 1st Cir. No. 05-2004 (dicta, Commerce Clause). Expect them
to be cited anyway.

## Remedy if MassTech *is* an arm of the state

**Official-capacity prospective relief against the chair or executive
director** survives regardless — *Ex parte Young*; *Will*, 491 U.S. at 71 n.10;
fees under § 1988.

This matters for how a right-to-record or § 20(f) claim gets framed from the
outset: injunctive and declaratory relief against named officials in their
official capacities is the posture that does not depend on winning the
arm-of-state question. Entity damages and *Monell* do.
